Story and World Design Document
Premise

A lone adventurer arrives in a remote desert settlement known as Dustfall. The town sits at the end of a neglected trade route and is surrounded by endless dunes. For generations, its people have lived under the shadow of a gigantic camel-like monster that emerges from the desert once every month.

The creature demands tribute in the form of food, livestock, tools, and other resources. Any refusal is met with destruction. The townspeople have complied for centuries.

Now the town faces its final month. Food stores are nearly depleted. If tribute is paid again, many residents will starve. If tribute is refused, the monster will attack.

The adventurer becomes involved in the town's struggle and must decide whether to leave, survive, or uncover the truth behind the creature.

Unknown to the townspeople, the monster is not a living beast. It is an ancient machine that has operated autonomously for centuries.

Setting
The Desert

The world outside Dustfall is almost entirely irrelevant to the story. The game focuses on a small region surrounding the town.

The desert is harsh, dry, and mostly empty. Winds constantly reshape the dunes. Travel is difficult and dangerous.

At night, small monsters emerge from hiding places among the rocks and sand.

The only route into town is an old trade road that receives few travelers.

Dustfall

Dustfall is a small frontier settlement inspired by fantasy and Wild West aesthetics.

Buildings are primarily wood and stone. Dirt roads connect the town's structures. Horses, wagons, and simple magical tools are common.

The town's entire identity revolves around surviving the monthly tribute.

Residents rarely speak about the future because few believe conditions will ever improve.

Atmosphere

Dustfall should feel:

Isolated
Tired
Suspicious of strangers
Resigned to its fate
Slowly losing hope

Many residents no longer believe the monster can be defeated.

History

No one knows when the monster first appeared.

The oldest stories claim it has existed longer than the town itself.

Generations ago, settlers attempted to fight it directly. Every attempt ended in disaster.

Over time, resistance was abandoned and tribute became tradition.

Parents teach children how much food must be surrendered each month.

Many residents have never questioned whether the creature is truly a beast.

Several old records have been lost or destroyed over the centuries.

As a result, much of the town's history has become myth rather than fact.

The Monster
Public Knowledge

The creature is known simply as "The Collector."

Descriptions vary slightly, but all agree on several details:

Enormous size
Camel-like appearance
Emerges from the desert once per month
Takes resources
Leaves afterward
Nearly impossible to harm

The Collector rarely attacks unless resisted.

Its arrival is treated almost like a natural disaster.

Reality

The Collector is an ancient machine.

Centuries of sand, erosion, and accumulated debris have disguised its true nature.

Most visible metal components are hidden beneath layers of hardened sediment.

Many unusual behaviors that townspeople interpret as animal behavior are actually automated functions.

The monthly tribute collection is part of a forgotten purpose that no longer makes sense in the modern world.

The machine continues operating because nobody has ever reached its internal control systems.

Main Characters
The Adventurer

The player character.

A traveler who arrives shortly before the next tribute.

Their past remains largely undefined.

They begin as an outsider with no ties to the town.

Through their actions, they may become a trusted member of the community or remain an isolated stranger.

The Sheriff

The sheriff serves as Dustfall's primary authority figure.

Practical and cautious.

The sheriff's responsibility is protecting the town, but years of failure have convinced them that survival is more important than heroism.

They initially discourage reckless attempts to challenge the monster.

Their greatest fear is inspiring hope only to lead people to their deaths.

The Shopkeeper

Owner of the town's small magic shop.

Elderly, short-tempered, and easily frustrated.

Many residents dismiss them as a cranky old merchant.

In reality, they were once a capable adventurer who attempted to investigate the monster years ago.

The experience left them physically weakened and emotionally bitter.

They possess knowledge that nobody else in town remembers.

The Bird-Person

A traveler from a distant land.

Their wings were permanently damaged during a desert accident.

Unable to fly and unable to leave safely, they have become stranded in Dustfall.

Unlike most residents, they have no ancestral connection to the town's traditions.

Because they view local customs from an outsider's perspective, they are more willing to question accepted beliefs.

The Twin Girls

Young sisters who spend most of their time exploring the town.

They are curious, observant, and frequently underestimated by adults.

They distrust outsiders and especially non-human races due to prejudices inherited from their family.

Despite this, they often discover information nobody else notices.

Their perspective gradually changes depending on their interactions with the player.

Story Structure
Act One: Arrival

The adventurer reaches Dustfall.

The town is preparing for the next tribute.

Residents are tense and resources are visibly scarce.

The player learns:

The existence of The Collector.
The town's history of tribute.
The approaching deadline.
The worsening food crisis.

Most people believe there is no solution.

The central question becomes:

"What exactly is the monster, and can it truly be defeated?"

Act Two: Investigation

The player becomes involved with townspeople and their problems.

By helping residents, trust is earned and new information becomes available.

The investigation reveals inconsistencies in local legends.

Strange discoveries begin to accumulate:

Unusual sounds.
Impossible physical features.
Ancient remnants buried beneath the sand.
Contradictions in historical accounts.

The player gradually realizes that the monster may not be a living creature.

Near the end of the act, enough evidence exists to strongly suggest an artificial origin.

Act Three: The Final Tribute

The Collector arrives.

Panic spreads throughout town.

The player must act using everything learned during the investigation.

If the truth was discovered, the player identifies a way to enter the machine and reach its control systems.

Inside, they confront the reality of what the machine truly is and why it continues operating.

The final objective is deactivation rather than destruction.

Side Stories
The Shopkeeper's Regret

The shopkeeper reveals details of a failed investigation from decades earlier.

Their personal struggle centers on guilt and missed opportunities.

Helping them allows old wounds to heal and unlocks forgotten knowledge.

The Bird-Person's Future

The bird-person struggles with isolation and loss of freedom.

Their story focuses on adaptation, identity, and finding purpose despite circumstances.

The Sheriff's Burden

The sheriff carries responsibility for every resident.

Their story explores leadership, fear, and the cost of protecting others.

The Twins' Perspective

The twins represent the next generation of Dustfall.

Their story examines inherited prejudice and whether fear must continue indefinitely.

Themes
Fear of the Unknown

The town has accepted a terrifying situation largely because nobody understands it.

Tradition Versus Truth

Many customs exist solely because previous generations followed them.

The story questions whether tradition should be accepted without examination.

Hope Versus Resignation

Most residents have abandoned hope.

The player's actions challenge that mindset.

Community

The town survives because its residents depend on one another.

Individual stories contribute to a larger narrative about collective survival.

Ending 1: The Town Freed (True Ending)
Requirements
Discover the true nature of The Collector.
Reach its interior.
Successfully deactivate it.
Outcome

The monthly tribute cycle ends.

Dustfall survives and begins rebuilding.

The townspeople must confront the fact that many of their beliefs were incorrect.

Theme

Knowledge over superstition.

Ending 2: Last Stand (Failure)
Requirements
Fail to discover the machine's true nature.
Organize a direct defense against The Collector.
Outcome

The townspeople and player fight bravely.

Weapons, magic, and courage prove insufficient.

The Collector continues functioning exactly as it always has.

The player dies during the battle.

The fate of the remaining town is left uncertain.

Theme

Courage alone cannot solve every problem.

Ending 3: Death in the Desert
Requirements
Die during exploration, combat, or a major mistake.
Outcome

The story ends immediately.

Dustfall continues toward its inevitable crisis without the player.

Theme

The world does not revolve around the protagonist.

Additional World Building
Daily Life in Dustfall

Life in Dustfall revolves around preparation for the next tribute.

Every resident measures time in months rather than years.

People rarely ask:

"What will happen next year?"

Instead they ask:

"Will we make it to the next collection?"

Children grow up watching carts loaded with supplies disappear into the desert.

Many residents have never tasted certain foods because those goods are always surrendered first.

Some citizens secretly believe the tribute keeps the town alive and that attempting to stop it will anger forces beyond their understanding.

Others quietly wonder whether their ancestors made a mistake centuries ago.

Local Beliefs and Folklore

Generations of fear have produced countless stories about The Collector.

Most are false.

Some contain fragments of truth.

Common tales include:

The Collector can smell lies.
The Collector remembers every person it has ever seen.
The Collector chooses who will die during bad harvests.
The Collector was sent by the gods.
The Collector sleeps beneath the largest dune in the desert.

Children dare one another to walk outside town at night and listen for its footsteps.

Many adults claim they have heard those footsteps even when the creature was nowhere nearby.

Hidden History

Long before Dustfall existed, another civilization occupied the region.

Very little remains.

Occasionally, windstorms uncover fragments of stone, metal, or strange artifacts.

Most people ignore these discoveries.

The desert has always produced mysteries.

The old shopkeeper is one of the few residents who believes these remnants are connected to The Collector.

No complete records survive.

Only scattered clues remain.

The Original Purpose of The Collector

The machine was never designed to terrorize settlements.

Long ago it served a practical purpose.

It was part of a forgotten system responsible for gathering resources and transporting them elsewhere.

The civilization that built it disappeared centuries ago.

The destinations it once served no longer exist.

The machine continues following instructions that no longer make sense.

From its perspective, nothing is wrong.

The world changed.

It did not.

The Mystery of Dustfall

An important question exists beneath the surface of the story:

Why did people settle here in the first place?

Most residents assume their ancestors were stubborn or desperate.

The truth is more complicated.

Early settlers arrived because the machine unintentionally created opportunities for survival.

Whenever it collected resources, traces of water and fertile soil occasionally surfaced from beneath the desert.

This made settlement possible.

Over generations, people forgot the connection.

The town came to view The Collector as its curse.

In a strange way, it was also the reason the town existed at all.

Town Districts
Main Street

The central road running through Dustfall.

Contains most businesses and public buildings.

Residents gather here for announcements, arguments, celebrations, and emergencies.

The street feels noticeably quieter as the tribute date approaches.

Residential Quarter

A cluster of modest homes.

Many buildings have been repaired repeatedly over generations.

Families often pass houses down through multiple generations.

Some homes contain old heirlooms and stories connected to the town's past.

The Water Tower

The town's most valuable structure.

Protecting the water supply is a constant concern.

Many local disputes ultimately trace back to water shortages.

The sheriff treats threats against the water supply as serious crimes.

The Old Stable

Partially abandoned.

Few animals remain because many were surrendered during previous tributes.

The stable serves as a gathering place for rumors and informal conversations.

Desert Perimeter

The area immediately beyond town.

Residents rarely travel farther than necessary.

Old markers and warning signs stand at irregular intervals.

Nobody remembers who originally placed them.

Additional Characters
The Undertaker

One of the oldest residents.

Calm, patient, and unsettlingly comfortable discussing death.

The undertaker has buried victims of the tribute system for decades.

They have become skeptical of legends and emotional reactions.

Because they see the consequences directly, they possess a practical view of the town's situation.

They often provide uncomfortable truths that others avoid.

The Schoolteacher

Responsible for educating the town's children.

They struggle with deciding how much truth to tell the next generation.

Some lessons preserve local myths because those stories have become part of the town's identity.

The teacher worries that teaching blind obedience helped create the current situation.

The Farmer

Represents the town's growing desperation.

Each month there is less to harvest.

Their fields are failing.

They measure the crisis in practical terms:

Not myths.

Not legends.

Food.

The farmer becomes increasingly vocal as the tribute approaches.

Escalation During the Story

As the deadline approaches, conditions worsen.

Early Period

Residents are nervous but functional.

Most routines continue normally.

People still joke and socialize.

Middle Period

Arguments become more common.

Food rationing begins.

Trust between neighbors starts to weaken.

Rumors spread rapidly.

Final Period

The town becomes divided.

Some residents want to flee.

Some want to fight.

Some want to continue paying tribute.

The approaching arrival of The Collector dominates every conversation.

Even small disagreements become more dangerous.

The Interior of The Collector

For centuries, nobody has entered the machine and survived long enough to understand it.

Inside, the atmosphere contrasts sharply with the dusty town.

The interior feels ancient, silent, and preserved.

Mechanical systems continue operating despite their age.

The architecture appears completely unlike anything found in Dustfall.

This should feel like stepping into another world hidden inside the familiar monster.

The discovery confirms that the creature was never alive.

Everything the town believed was built upon misunderstanding.

Epilogue

If the machine is deactivated, Dustfall enters a period of uncertainty.

The monthly tribute no longer exists.

For the first time in centuries, the town must make decisions without fear guiding them.

Some residents celebrate.

Some struggle to adapt.

Many realize their entire understanding of history was wrong.

The future remains unwritten.

The final tone should be hopeful but realistic.

The monster's defeat solves the immediate crisis.

It does not magically solve every problem.

Instead, it gives the town something it has not possessed for generations:

A chance to choose its own destiny.
